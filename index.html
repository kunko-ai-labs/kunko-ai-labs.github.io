Judge Audit — the Moody's for AI judges

AI judges — LLM-as-a-judge systems and probabilistic decision APIs — now decide what gets shipped, filtered, moderated and approved. But a judge that says "90% confident" while being right 60% of the time is a liability. Judge Audit is the independent harness that audits judge calibration: does a judge's stated confidence match its real accuracy? Install it, point it at any judge plus a dataset with known-correct answers, and get the statistics that matter.

Under the hood

What we actually measure

A judge audit answers one question six ways: can you trust this judge's confidence?

  • ECE — Expected Calibration ErrorA single number for "how far is confidence from reality". Reported with confidence intervals, not point estimates.
  • Reliability diagramsSee where the judge lies: overconfident at the top, underconfident at the bottom, or honest throughout.
  • Accuracy–coverage curvesWhat can you safely automate at what error rate? The automation frontier, quantified.
  • Adversarial robustnessPrompt injection, homoglyphs, social engineering: does the judge hold its calibration under attack?
  • Cost & latencyCalibration per dollar and per millisecond — because assurance has a budget too.
  • Deliberation auditsWhen judges debate, does agreement mean truth? We measure false consensus, not just consensus.

FAQ

Questions we get

What does "calibration" mean, in plain language?

If an AI judge says it's 90% confident across 100 decisions, it should be right about 90 times. If it's right 60 times, it's miscalibrated — overconfident. Calibration is the match between stated confidence and actual accuracy, and it's what makes a judge safe to automate around.

Is Judge Audit itself another AI judge?

No. Judge Audit judges nothing — it measures. Given a judge and a dataset with known-correct answers, it computes statistics. That's arithmetic, not opinion, and every number is reproducible from raw checkpoints in the repo.

Why does this matter for the EU AI Act?

High-risk AI systems (in force December 2027) need logging, human oversight, and demonstrated accuracy and robustness. Independent calibration evidence is exactly the kind of documentation those obligations call for.

How do I run an audit?

pip install kunko-judge-audit, bring your judge and a labeled dataset, and follow the quickstart in the repo. Pre-register your hypotheses if you want the audit to count as evidence.
